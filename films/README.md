# Films

Two five-minute comedies, written, staged, animated and mixed in code, with
every voice and most effects generated by ElevenLabs.

| Film | Runtime | Style | File |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Starting Monday** | 5:31 | Sci-fi animated comedy | `out/starting-monday.mp4` |
| **Un-Bless You** | 5:08 | Sketch comedy, staged as a live-action animatic | `out/un-bless-you.mp4` |

Both are 1920x1080, 24 fps, H.264 with AAC stereo, and carry English
subtitles as a selectable track.

## Starting Monday

Dale Krupnik has paid for a gym membership for eleven years and never gone.
He's finally on hold to cancel it. Then reality tears open in his living
room: two auditors from the Galactic Reserve have come to stop him. Dale's
perfectly reliable inertia is what backs the galaxy's currency, and nine
trillion pensions depend on him never doing a single push-up. Then a
personal trainer turns up who has bet everything on Dale improving.

The comic engine is that everyone treats Dale's laziness as a precious
natural resource, and Dale works out how much leverage that gives him. He
spends it on a parking space and on Greg being upset. Callbacks: the hold
music never stops (it plays from the phone under the whole film), Kayla's
recorded voice comes back at the worst moment of the push-up, and the
trainer's real body ends up on Dale's exercise bike in the stinger.

| Brad / Vex | Charlie | a trainer suit with a slug inside |
| Kayla | Jessica, phone filter | FlexZone's hold recording |
| Sharon | Sarah | has said she's leaving 4,000 times |

## Un-Bless You

Marcus, on the phone before his big presentation, sneezes at a bus stop.
Leonard, an old man doing the crossword, says "bless you" and waits for a
thank-you that doesn't come. So he takes the blessing back. He draws it out
of Marcus's chest, folds it and pockets it. Marcus's day comes apart in
public, he has to earn the blessing back through paperwork, and he can't
re-qualify until he sneezes again. Then Leonard sneezes, on the bus step,
and the power changes hands.

It is built as a sketch-show pattern. A courtesy is treated as a contract,
and each escalation makes the contract more bureaucratic: re-blessing
fees, three thank-yous (one in advance), eye contact required. The reversal
pays off a setup from the first minute, the one-finger "one sec". The tag
belongs to the bystander who hasn't spoken for five minutes.

| Character | Voice |
|---|---|
| Marcus | Liam |
| Leonard | Bill |
| Denise | Matilda |
| Mum | Alice |
| Marcus's mom | Bella, phone filter |

The second brief asked for live action. Nothing was filmed: it is a 2D
animatic staged the way a sketch show would shoot it. That means a locked-off
wide, singles and over-the-shoulders, a 2.39:1 letterbox, a camera that drifts
slightly, depth-of-field blur, flat colour and hairline outlines. The picture
grammar and the sound mix (street ambience, foley, almost no score) are
deliberately the opposite of the first film's.

## How it is made

```
script.mjs ──► generate-audio.mjs ──► audio/vo/*.mp3, audio/sfx/*.mp3    (ElevenLabs)
                                          │
                         mix.py ◄─────────┘   lays out the timeline from the real takes,
                           │                  mixes the soundtrack with sound.py
                           ├──► build/timeline.json, build/mix.wav, build/captions.srt
                           ▼
scene.js on engine/*.js ──► render.mjs ──► out/<slug>.mp4               (headless Chromium + ffmpeg)
```

- **`<slug>/script.mjs`** is the shooting script as data: the cast, the voice
  for each role, and the beats. A beat is a line of dialogue (with performance
  directions in brackets) or a timed silence with named marks.
- **`scripts/generate-audio.mjs`** sends each line to ElevenLabs text-to-speech
  (`eleven_v4`) and each prompted effect to sound generation
  (`eleven_text_to_sound_v3`). Results are cached against a hash of their
  inputs, so a re-run only pays for what changed.
- **`scripts/mix.py`** times every beat from the recorded audio, so the
  timeline is whatever the performances make it. It writes per-frame mouth
  levels for lip sync, mixes dialogue, effects, music and ambience on ducking
  buses, and normalises to -16 LUFS. **`<slug>/sound.py`** is that film's
  sound design.
- **`engine/`** is a deterministic `renderAt(t)` over SVG: keyframe tracks,
  easing, a camera, and `rig.js`, a poseable two-bone-IK character with a
  parametric face. **`<slug>/scene.js`** stages the film, cueing every shot
  off beat ids.
- **`scripts/render.mjs`** steps the scene frame by frame in headless
  Chromium and encodes the frames with the mix and the subtitles.

### Rebuild

```bash
npm install
python3 -m venv .venv && . .venv/bin/activate && pip install numpy scipy
npm run films:audio -- starting-monday --dry   # what would be generated, and its cost
npm run films:audio -- starting-monday         # needs ELEVENLABS_API_KEY
npm run films:mix -- starting-monday
npm run films:render -- starting-monday        # about 15 minutes for a full film
npm run films:render -- starting-monday --stills 30,95,250 --width 800   # a contact sheet
```

You also need `ffmpeg`. Playwright's Chromium does the rendering.

`ELEVENLABS_API_KEY` is read from the environment, or from `.env.local`,
which is gitignored. In a Claude Code cloud session the key is stored as a
network secret instead, and the proxy adds it to requests, so no variable
holds it. There, run generation with a placeholder variable and Node's
proxy support:
`ELEVENLABS_API_KEY=proxy-injected NODE_USE_ENV_PROXY=1 npm run films:audio -- <slug>`.

The generated audio is committed, so mixing and rendering need no key.
Producing both films used about 7,900 of the free plan's 10,000 monthly
credits.
