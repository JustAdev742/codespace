SIL mandatory registration: the 1 October 2026 rules in one page

Summarised from the NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commission's published transition pathways and the NDIA's SIL claiming notice, as at September 2026. General information, not legal advice. Confirm at ndiscommission.gov.au.

Who is captured

Providers delivering supported independent living: support at all times of the day or most of the day, helping a person live in their home as autonomously as possible, where the provider manages and delivers the SIL package. A few hours a day, or supports the participant self-directs and rosters, is not SIL. STA, MTA and ILO stay under group 0115 where registration remains optional.

Dates

  • 1 July 2026 — registration group 0138 and the SIL Practice Standards module commenced. Registered 0115 providers are varied to 0138 and audited against the SIL module at their next mid-term or renewal audit.
  • 1 October 2026 — providers already delivering SIL who have not lodged an application must stop delivering SIL. Those who have applied may continue while the application is assessed.
  • New entrants after 1 July cannot deliver SIL until registered.

What "applied" means

A valid application in the NDIS Commission Applications Portal (PRODA login): organisation and key personnel details, the registration groups you deliver (0138 plus any others), and a self-assessment against the Core Module and the SIL supplementary module with supporting evidence. An application must be completed within 60 days of being started. There is no application fee; the audit is the cost.

What happens after lodgement

The Commission issues an audit scope; you obtain quotes from an Approved Quality Auditor (19 on the Commission's list; several are booked 6–8 weeks out); a certification audit follows (Stage 1 desktop, Stage 2 on-site with participant and worker interviews); non-conformities are closed; the Commission decides suitability. Typical single-site SIL audit fees are A$7,000–15,000.

Consequences

  • Delivering SIL without registration or a lodged application: maximum 2 years' imprisonment, 120 penalty units (A$43,680), or both.
  • From 1 October plan managers are directed to reject invoices from SIL providers that are neither registered nor applied; non-applicants can claim only for services to 30 September 2026.

The Commission's warning about consultants and template packs

Applicants who use a consultant or purchased policies must have been substantially involved in preparing the application. Consultants supplying the same or very similar documents to multiple providers may indicate a lack of understanding and can lead to refusal or additional audit conditions. False or misleading information in an application attracts civil and criminal penalties.

The four SIL module outcomes auditors will test

  1. Supported decision-making
  2. Safeguarding in the home
  3. Practice governance and workforce consistency
  4. Agreements about tenancy, housing and support (service agreement separate from tenancy; changing support provider must not cost the participant their home)
