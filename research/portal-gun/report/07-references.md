## How this list was made, and how far to trust it

- **Source.** Every entry comes from the seven research notes written for this study
  ([`notes/`](notes/)): 01 classical wormhole geometry; 02 energy conditions, QFT and the Casimir effect;
  03 topology change, causality, quantum gravity and the 2022 quantum-processor experiment; 04 experiments
  and analogues; 05 Rick and Morty canon; 06 prop hardware; 07 portal rendering. An agent compiled them into
  this list without looking anything up again. Where a note did not record a field (initials, pages, DOI), it
  is left out rather than filled in.
- **Access status.** Each entry repeats the note's own record of what was read:
  - *full text read*: the paper itself was read (sometimes only the sections named);
  - *abstract only*: only the abstract was available;
  - *bibliographic only*: the source was identified but not read.

  The report relies on a source only as far as it was read.
- **DOI checks.** Every DOI recorded in the notes (128) was resolved against Crossref on 2026-10-19; all
  resolve. For the 125 entries with a DOI in this list, the entry's year, volume and first page or article
  number were then compared with Crossref's metadata:
  - 121 matched;
  - 2 were false alarms (zero-padded JHEP article numbers);
  - 2 were corrected: a DOI pointing to a different article in the same issue (Friedman & Higuchi 2006),
    and an entry missing its title, year and volume (Zhang et al. 2023).

  The comparison script is in the session's scratch files; its result is recorded in
  [08-research-audit.md](08-research-audit.md).
- **Quotations** are short and in quotation marks; everything else paraphrases the notes. "Note:" introduces a
  correction, misprint, discrepancy, retraction or critique recorded in the notes.

## Classical wormhole geometry and traversability

- **Einstein, A. & Rosen, N. (1935).** The Particle Problem in the General Theory of Relativity. *Physical Review* 48(1), 73–77 (1 July 1935). <https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.48.73>. INSPIRE abstract query recorded in note 01: <https://inspirehep.net/api/literature?q=t%20%22particle%20problem%20in%20the%20general%20theory%20of%20relativity%22>. — *Access:* abstract only (notes 01 and 03; APS returned HTTP 403, abstract read via the INSPIRE API). — Proposes a static "bridge" joining two identical sheets of space as a singularity-free model of a particle, not as a route for travel; the bridge is the non-traversable Schwarzschild wormhole. Note: the coordinate substitution u² = r − 2m attributed to Einstein and Rosen comes from a secondary source (Weinstein, next entry), and the explicit bridge metric in note 01 is the note-writer's own derivation, not a transcription; Morris & Thorne 1988 credit Flamm (Physik. Z. 17, 448, 1916) with first recognising the wormhole character of the Schwarzschild solution.
- **Weinstein, G. (year not recorded).** Unveiling the Connection: ER Bridges and EPR in Einstein's Research. arXiv:2307.05548. — *Access:* full text read (note 01; used as a secondary source). — Historical study that note 01 relies on for the substitution u² = r − 2m that Einstein and Rosen used to build their bridge.
- **Wheeler (1955).** Geons. *Physical Review* 97, 511–536. <https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.97.511>. — *Access:* abstract only (note 03). — Introduces geons, self-gravitating electromagnetic entities offered as a classical, divergence-free model of "body", for masses of roughly 10^39 g to 10^57 g. Note: the often-repeated claim that this paper contains the first wormhole ("doubly connected space") diagram was not verified; the abstract does not mention wormholes.
- **Misner & Wheeler (1957).** Classical physics as geometry. *Ann. Phys. (N.Y.)* 2, 525–603. <https://doi.org/10.1016/0003-4916(57)90049-0>. — *Access:* abstract only (note 03). — "Charge without charge": electric charge is read as source-free field lines trapped in the wormholes of a multiply connected space, so the two mouths look oppositely charged; note 03 uses this as the starting point for what happens to a mouth's charge when matter passes through.
- **Kruskal, M. D. (1960).** Maximal Extension of Schwarzschild Metric. *Physical Review* 119(5), 1743–1745 (1 September 1960). <https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.119.1743>. — *Access:* not stated; note 01 records the citation as confirmed via Crossref (the paper is cited by Morris & Thorne 1988 and by Morris, Thorne & Yurtsever 1988). — Cited, with Fuller & Wheeler 1962, for the Schwarzschild throat pinching off too quickly for anything to cross. Note: the reference list of Morris & Thorne's AJP paper misprints it as Phys. Rev. 116, 1743 (checked on the scan); Morris, Thorne & Yurtsever cite it correctly.
- **Fuller, R. W. & Wheeler, J. A. (1962).** Causality and Multiply Connected Space-Time. *Physical Review* 128(2), 919–929 (15 October 1962). <https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.128.919>. — *Access:* abstract only (notes 01 and 03; APS 403, abstract read via the INSPIRE API). — Asks whether a signal through a wormhole could outrun light going the long way, and finds that the Schwarzschild throat pinches off in a finite time and traps the signal in a region of infinite curvature, so causality is preserved; this is the classic non-traversability result. Note: the paper's own equations (maximum throat size, pinch-off time) were not seen.
- **Ellis, H. G. (1973).** Ether flow through a drainhole: A particle model in general relativity. *Journal of Mathematical Physics* 14(1), 104–118 (January 1973; received 2 December 1969 per the AIP landing page). Erratum: *J. Math. Phys.* 15(4), 520 (1974). <https://doi.org/10.1063/1.1666161>. — *Access:* abstract only (note 01; full text paywalled, verbatim abstract read via the INSPIRE and Crossref APIs). — Coupling geometry to a scalar field of reversed polarity (a ghost scalar in modern terms) gives a static, spherically symmetric, horizonless and geodesically complete "drainhole" with positive mass on one side and negative mass on the other; its massless case is the metric used in Morris & Thorne's Box 2 and by James et al. 2015. Note: Shinkai & Hayward 2002 mis-cite it as "J. Math. Phys. 14, 395 (1973)" (the correct first page is 104); the explicit metric formulas in note 01 come from later authors, not from Ellis directly.
- **Bronnikov, K. A. (1973).** Scalar-tensor theory and scalar charge. *Acta Physica Polonica B* 4, 251–266. No DOI. — *Access:* bibliographic only / not accessed (note 01; title and pages confirmed via INSPIRE and the reference list of Gonzalez, Guzman & Sarbach 2009; the journal archive was unreachable, with an expired TLS certificate and HTTP 503, and TLS was not bypassed). — Credited, with Ellis 1973, for the Ellis–Bronnikov wormhole family; the attribution of explicit formulas to it rests on later authors.
- **Chetouani, L. & Clément, G. (1984).** Geometrical optics in the Ellis geometry. *General Relativity and Gravitation* 16(2), 111–119 (February 1984). <https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00762440>. — *Access:* abstract only (note 01; INSPIRE). — Computes the cross-section for light scattered by the Ellis geometry in the geometrical-optics approximation, with its two asymptotically flat regions identified; the deflection formula itself was not seen. Note: the exact deflection formula confirmed in note 01 comes from Nakajima & Asada 2012, not from this paper.
- **Clement, G. (1984).** *Int. J. Theor. Phys.* 23, 335. — *Access:* not checked (note 01). — Cited by Nakajima & Asada 2012, together with Chetouani & Clément, for early work on light scattering by the Ellis wormhole.
- **Morris, M. S. & Thorne, K. S. (1988).** Wormholes in spacetime and their use for interstellar travel: A tool for teaching general relativity. *American Journal of Physics* 56(5), 395–412 (May 1988; received 16 March 1987, accepted 17 July 1987). <https://doi.org/10.1119/1.15620>. Publisher page (paywalled): <https://pubs.aip.org/ajp/article/56/5/395/1044276/>. Internet Archive scan: <https://archive.org/details/pdfy--5NLGQyfpB61dmyG>. — *Access:* note 01: full text read (every equation it quotes was read from the page images of a third-party Internet Archive scan whose footers match the published pages; cite the journal, not the scan); note 04 lists "Morris & Thorne 1988" as bibliographic only, without further details. — The founding traversable-wormhole paper: it defines the static spherical metric (redshift and shape functions, flare-out at the throat), shows that the throat needs "exotic" material whose tension exceeds its energy density, about 5×10^41 dyn/cm² × (10 m / throat radius)², and sets traveller-comfort limits (about one Earth gravity of acceleration and of tidal stretch across 2 m, a trip under a year), with worked examples ranging from a "comfortable hour" to about 200 days. Note: the Casimir plate-separation estimate often credited to this paper is in Morris, Thorne & Yurtsever 1988 (here only a note added in proof points to it); Box 2's metric is the massless Ellis/Bronnikov wormhole, although the authors say they know of no previous studies; the "exoticity" function is defined in two versions, with and without an absolute value, which matters for the sign in the Casimir note; the Kruskal reference is misprinted (116 for 119); a web-search summary's figure of 4.8×10^40 for the throat tension is wrong, and the paper's 10^41 is right.
- **Morris, M. S., Thorne, K. S. & Yurtsever, U. (1988).** Wormholes, Time Machines, and the Weak Energy Condition. *Physical Review Letters* 61(13), 1446–1449 (26 September 1988; received 21 June 1988). <https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.61.1446>. CaltechAUTHORS copy: <https://authors.library.caltech.edu/records/m644f-tbz27> (file MORprl88.pdf). — *Access:* full text read (note 01: scanned CaltechAUTHORS PDF, all four pages read as images; note 03: same record, key passages checked on high-resolution page images). — Shows that keeping a wormhole open requires violating the averaged weak energy condition along some null geodesic (a sphere round one mouth is an outer trapped surface seen from the other side); gives the Casimir-plate model, with plates about 10^-10 cm apart for a throat radius of 1 AU and a possible need for a separation below the electron Compton wavelength, "which might well be forbidden"; and shows how moving one mouth turns the wormhole into a time machine, arguing classically that its Cauchy horizon may be stable. It also says classical creation would need closed timelike curves and/or a non-continuous choice of light cone plus WEC violation, or "pulling" a wormhole out of the quantum foam. Note: it prints the Planck–Wheeler length as 1.3×10^-33 cm (the standard value is 1.6×10^-33 cm, which the AJP paper prints) and the far-field redshift as Φ ≃ −2M/|l|, which note 03 judges a probable factor-2 typo (the Schwarzschild limit gives −M/|l|); note 03's estimate that a 1 AU Casimir wormhole would weigh about 10^8 solar masses is its own arithmetic.
- **Visser, M. (1989a).** Traversable wormholes: Some simple examples. *Physical Review D* 39(10), 3182–3184 (15 May 1989). <https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.39.3182>. arXiv:0809.0907, <https://arxiv.org/abs/0809.0907> (the author's 2008 re-typeset upload). — *Access:* full text read (note 01; arXiv re-typeset version, so equation numbers are the reprint's; the journal pages were not seen). — Builds polyhedral "cut-and-paste" wormholes in flat space: a traveller crossing a flat face meets no matter and no tidal force and is simply shunted to the other side, but the edges must carry negative-tension strings with energy per unit length μ = T = −1/(8G) ≈ −1.5×10^43 J/m, independent of size, and Visser notes that no natural mechanism for negative string tension is known; stability was left open. The note-writer's own conversion (not in the paper) is about −1.7×10^26 kg per metre of edge, or about −2 Jupiter masses for a cube 2 m on a side.
- **Visser, M. (1989b).** Traversable wormholes from surgically modified Schwarzschild spacetimes. *Nuclear Physics B* 328(1), 203–212 (December 1989). <https://doi.org/10.1016/0550-3213(89)90100-4>. arXiv:0809.0927, <https://arxiv.org/abs/0809.0927> (the author's 2008 re-typeset upload). — *Access:* full text read (note 01; arXiv reprint, equation numbers as in the reprint). — Thin-shell wormholes made by gluing two Schwarzschild exteriors at a radius a > 2M: the shell has negative surface energy density, traceless surface matter (as for massless-field Casimir stress) gives no solution, and global stability against collapse needs a non-positive mass as seen from infinity, which Visser calls "alarming, but not necessarily fatal". Note: this paper uses a surface tension ϑ, while Poisson & Visser 1995 use a surface pressure p = −ϑ, so its equation-of-state parameter k² corresponds to β² = −k² there.
- **Poisson, E. & Visser, M. (1995).** Thin-shell wormholes: Linearization stability. *Physical Review D* 52(12), 7318–7321 (15 December 1995). <https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.52.7318>. arXiv:gr-qc/9506083, <https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9506083>. — *Access:* full text read (note 01; arXiv v1, equations checked on rendered pages; Fig. 1 is not in the arXiv PDF, only its caption). — Linear radial stability of thin-shell Schwarzschild wormholes: if the parameter β0 is read as a sound speed between 0 and 1 they are always unstable, and stable cases need an exotic equation of state on top of negative surface energy (β0² ≤ −1/2 for large throats, or β0² ≥ 3/2 + √3 ≈ 3.23 for 2M < a0 < 3M). Note: the authors do not declare stability impossible, because β0 need not be a sound speed and |β0²| > 1 should not be ruled out a priori.
- **Visser, M. (1995).** Lorentzian Wormholes: From Einstein to Hawking. AIP Series in Computational and Applied Mathematical Physics. *American Institute of Physics / AIP Press, Woodbury, NY*, 412 pp. ISBN 1-56396-394-9 (hardcover; 978-1-56396-394-0); paperback 1996, ISBN 1-56396-653-0 (978-1-56396-653-8); LC call no. QC173.6 .V57 1995. Open Library records: <https://openlibrary.org/isbn/1563963949>, <https://openlibrary.org/isbn/1563966530>. — *Access:* bibliographic only / not accessed (notes 01 and 03; the book was not read). — The standard monograph on traversable wormholes. Note 03 records that it is the usual citation for the "accepted" bookkeeping in which matter passing through changes the mouths' masses and charges, so that bookkeeping could not be checked at primary level. Note: it is cited as "AIP Press (1995)" by Visser, Kar & Dadhich 2003 and as "American Institute of Physics, New York, 1995" by Poisson & Visser 1995 and Hochberg & Visser 1998; no Springer reprint ISBN could be confirmed, and its section numbers were not verified.
- **Hochberg, D. & Visser, M. (1997).** Geometric structure of the generic static traversable wormhole throat. *Physical Review D* 56(8), 4745–4755 (15 October 1997). <https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.56.4745>. arXiv:gr-qc/9704082, <https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9704082>. — *Access:* full text read (note 01; arXiv v1 text extraction, key equations read). — Defines a throat of any shape as a surface of minimal area, generalises the Morris–Thorne flare-out condition, and shows that the null energy condition is generically violated at some points on any static throat and, suitably weighted, on average over it; throats of genus two or more must contain regions of negative energy density. Static spacetimes only; the 1998 paper removes that restriction.
- **Hochberg, D. & Visser, M. (1998).** Null Energy Condition in Dynamic Wormholes. *Physical Review Letters* 81(4), 746–749 (27 July 1998); arXiv title "The null energy condition in dynamic wormholes". <https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.81.746>. arXiv:gr-qc/9802048 (v3), <https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9802048>. — *Access:* full text read (note 01; arXiv v3). — Without assuming any symmetry, asymptotic flatness or time independence, it shows that NEC violation occurs at or near the throat of anything that deserves to be called a traversable wormhole, and that a time-dependent wormhole has two throats, one for each direction of travel; claims of NEC-respecting wormholes are sorted into semantic games, genuine ambiguities and errors.
- **Teo, E. (1998).** Rotating traversable wormholes. *Physical Review D* 58(2), 024014 (24 June 1998). <https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.58.024014>. arXiv:gr-qc/9803098 (v2). — *Access:* full text read (key sections via text extraction) (note 01). — Extends the Morris–Thorne metric to stationary, axisymmetric, rotating wormholes: the NEC is still violated somewhere on the throat, but the exotic matter can be moved so that some infalling observers never meet it, although "one can never avoid the use of exotic matter altogether". An illustrative model only, with no stability analysis and no treatment of tidal "engineering" issues.
- **Armendariz-Picon, C. (2002).** On a class of stable, traversable Lorentzian wormholes in classical general relativity. *Physical Review D* 65, 104010. <https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.65.104010>. arXiv:gr-qc/0201027. — *Access:* abstract only (note 01). — Claimed an analytic proof that the zero-mass ghost-scalar (Ellis) wormhole is stable. Note: superseded; Gonzalez, Guzman & Sarbach 2009 explain that the proof covered only perturbations that vanish at the throat ("placing a mirror at the throat") and prove the wormhole linearly unstable, while Nandi et al. 2006 still repeat the stability claim.
- **Shinkai, H. & Hayward, S. A. (2002).** Fate of the first traversible wormhole: Black-hole collapse or inflationary expansion. *Physical Review D* 66(4), 044005 (16 August 2002). <https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.66.044005>. arXiv:gr-qc/0205041 (v2), <https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0205041>. — *Access:* note 01: full text read (arXiv v2 via text extraction; figures not inspected); note 03: abstract only. — The first numerical study of the Ellis (Morris–Thorne Box 2) wormhole: small pulses make it collapse to a black hole if the net input energy is positive and inflate if it is negative, so normal matter such as a traveller always produces a black hole, and carefully balanced extra ghost radiation keeps it open only "for a limited time", with fine tuning. Note: its reference list mis-cites Ellis as "J. Math. Phys. 14, 395 (1973)" (the correct page is 104), and its acceptance of linear stability was overturned by Gonzalez, Guzman & Sarbach 2009.
- **Hayward (2002).** Black holes and traversible wormholes: a synthesis. arXiv:gr-qc/0203051 (unpublished per INSPIRE). — *Access:* abstract only (note 03). — A two-dimensional dilaton-gravity model covering the construction of wormholes from black holes, their use for transport including back-reaction, their maintenance, and their collapse.
- **Perlick, V. (2004).** Exact gravitational lens equation in spherically symmetric and static spacetimes. *Physical Review D* 69(6), 064017 (17 March 2004); the arXiv title begins "On the exact gravitational lens equation...". <https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.69.064017>. arXiv:gr-qc/0307072 (v2). — *Access:* full text read (Ellis section, Sec. V B, pp. 7–9) (note 01). — For an observer on one side of an Ellis wormhole, light from sources on the far side fills a disk of angular radius δ with sin²δ = a²/(r_O² + a²), rimmed by infinitely many Einstein rings; note 01 reads this (its own interpretation) as the mouth looking like a disk the size of the throat, which is the "portal" image.
- **Hayward, S. A. & Koyama, H. (2004).** How to make a traversable wormhole from a Schwarzschild black hole. *Physical Review D* 70(10), 101502(R) (22 November 2004). <https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.70.101502>. arXiv:gr-qc/0406080. — *Access:* full text read (notes 01 and 03; arXiv v1). — Beaming impulsive phantom (negative-energy) radiation into the two-sided eternal Schwarzschild solution leaves a static traversable wormhole with throat radius a = 2(M + μ), smaller than the original horizon; a negative pulse followed by a positive one enlarges it, and the reverse order shrinks it. Note: the authors' own caveats are that the result is not asymptotically flat, that it needs the already two-sided eternal topology because classical topology change requires causal loops, that black holes formed by stellar collapse "would presumably not have the appropriate topology", and that the phantom radiation is an idealised exotic-matter model.
- **Koyama & Hayward (2004).** Construction and enlargement of traversable wormholes from Schwarzschild black holes. *Physical Review D* 70, 084001. arXiv:gr-qc/0406113. — *Access:* abstract only (note 03). — The long version of Hayward & Koyama 2004, with the Barrabès–Israel matching.
- **Susskind, L. (2005a).** Wormholes and time travel? Not likely. arXiv:gr-qc/0503097 (v3); not peer-reviewed (INSPIRE lists no journal). — *Access:* full text read (note 03). — A 2+1-dimensional heuristic: the flux of a charge passed through threads back through the wormhole, so the entrance mouth gains the traveller's charge and mass and the exit mouth loses the same, with the totals seen from each side unchanged; Susskind first concluded that the transit time is then completely uncertain, which would make the wormhole useless. Note: the rebuttal below withdraws the uncertainty conclusion but not the mass and charge bookkeeping.
- **Susskind, L. (2005b).** Rebuttal to a paper on wormholes. arXiv:gr-qc/0504039; not peer-reviewed. — *Access:* full text read (note 03). — Self-rebuttal: the "useless" conclusion assumed mouths in energy eigenstates, and with a well-defined conjugate variable coherence survives; Susskind still states a personal "prejudice" that wormholes do not make sense.
- **Nandi, K. K., Zhang, Y.-Z. & Zakharov, A. V. (2006).** Gravitational lensing by wormholes. *Physical Review D* 74(2), 024020 (20 July 2006). <https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.74.024020>. arXiv:gr-qc/0602062 (v4). — *Access:* full text (Ellis section read via text extraction) (note 01). — Gives the exact Ellis deflection angle in terms of the closest-approach distance as an elliptic integral, which note 01's own check finds equivalent to the Nakajima & Asada formula. Note: Nakajima & Asada warn that the elliptic-integral argument used here is the square of the usual modulus, and the statement that the zero-mass Ellis wormhole "is a stable configuration" (citing Armendariz-Picon 2002) is superseded.
- **Krasnikov (2008).** Electrostatic interaction of a pointlike charge with a wormhole. *Classical and Quantum Gravity* 25, 245018. arXiv:0802.1358. — *Access:* abstract only (note 03). — All source-free electrostatic potentials around a wormhole differ only in the flux through the throat, which can therefore be read as the wormhole's "charge" in Wheeler's sense, and the charge feels a self-force, computed in the short-throat limit. Note: the abstract says the self-force result disagrees with Khusnutdinov–Bakhmatov.
- **Gonzalez, J. A., Guzman, F. S. & Sarbach, O. (2009a).** Instability of wormholes supported by a ghost scalar field: I. Linear stability analysis. *Classical and Quantum Gravity* 26(1), 015010 (2009; online 16 December 2008). <https://doi.org/10.1088/0264-9381/26/1/015010>. arXiv:0806.0608 (v2), <https://arxiv.org/abs/0806.0608>. — *Access:* full text read (key sections) (note 01; arXiv v2). — Proves that every static Ellis–Bronnikov wormhole is linearly unstable, with exactly one growing mode on a time scale of about 0.6 to 0.85 times the throat's areal radius divided by c; in the authors' words a throat under about 1 km "decays in a few µs". Note 01's own conversion gives 2.8 ns for a 1 m throat, which is an e-folding time, not a total lifetime.
- **Gonzalez, J. A., Guzman, F. S. & Sarbach, O. (2009b).** "... II. Nonlinear evolution" (title as recorded in note 01: Part II of the series above). *Classical and Quantum Gravity* 26(1), 015011 (2009). <https://doi.org/10.1088/0264-9381/26/1/015011>. arXiv:0806.1370 (v2), <https://arxiv.org/abs/0806.1370>. — *Access:* abstract + introduction read (not studied in detail) (note 01). — The nonlinear runs confirm the instability: depending on the perturbation the wormhole either expands or collapses to a Schwarzschild black hole, on time scales that match linear theory, and in the collapsing case a light signal can still cross and come back before the black hole forms.
- **Hayward, S. A. (2009).** Wormhole dynamics in spherical symmetry. *Physical Review D* 79, 124001. <https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.79.124001>. arXiv:0903.5438. — *Access:* note 01: partially read (abstract; Secs. IV–V via text extraction); note 03: full text read (§§IV–V). — Treats wormhole mouths as trapping horizons and shows the NEC is necessarily violated at a mouth; at a static throat the surface-gravity condition reduces to Morris & Thorne's flare-out condition. Enlarging a wormhole needs negative energy followed by positive energy, and a wormhole "can collapse to a black hole if its negative-energy source fails" or if enough positive energy is pumped in.
- **Nakajima, K. & Asada, H. (2012).** Deflection angle of light in an Ellis wormhole geometry. *Physical Review D* 85, 107501. <https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.85.107501>. arXiv:1204.3710. — *Access:* full text read (note 01). — The clean exact result: deflection α(b) = 2K(k) − π with k = a/b (K the complete elliptic integral, modulus convention), weak-field limit (π/4)(a/b)² + (9π/64)(a/b)⁴, diverging logarithmically as the ray approaches the throat; the massless Ellis wormhole bends light even though it has zero mass. Note: Dey & Sen's approximate formula is right only at leading order.
- **James, O., von Tunzelmann, E., Franklin, P. & Thorne, K. S. (2015).** Visualizing Interstellar's Wormhole. *American Journal of Physics* 83(6), 486–499 (June 2015). <https://doi.org/10.1119/1.4916949>. arXiv:1502.03809 (v3), <https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.03809>; also on CaltechAUTHORS (record 103ed-m0n12). — *Access:* full text read (note 01; arXiv v3, equations read from rendered pages). — Introduces the three-parameter "Dneg" wormhole and the null-geodesic ray-tracing equations behind the film's images, and describes what a mouth looks like: a sphere showing a lensed image of the other universe, thin extra images near its edge and an Einstein ring just outside it. It also says a wormhole would pinch off without exotic matter at its throat and that quantum inequalities likely forbid macroscopic ones, "Likely, but not certain." Note: the phrase "crystal ball" does not occur in the paper (searched for), and the geometry was chosen for visual and narrative reasons, ignoring constraints on the required stress-energy.
- **Lobo (2016).** *IJMPD* 25, 1630017 (a review). arXiv:1604.02082. — *Access:* abstract only (note 01; used as a secondary source). — The review's abstract states that the throat's flaring-out condition, through the Einstein field equation, entails violation of the null energy condition.
- **Gratus, Kinsler & McCall (2019).** Evaporating black-holes, wormholes, and vacuum polarisation: must they always conserve charge? *Found. Phys.* 49, 330–350. <https://doi.org/10.1007/s10701-019-00251-5>. arXiv:1904.04103. — *Access:* full text read (§V) (note 03). — Describes the accepted view that a charge passing through a wormhole leaves an effective charge on the mouths (±nq after n transits), and proposes instead, using gauge freedom in the excitation fields, that a mouth's effective charge depends only on how far the charge currently is from the throat, not on its transit history. Note: this is a non-standard, untested modification of how Maxwell theory is treated.

## Energy conditions, exotic matter, Casimir effect and quantum inequalities

- **Casimir, H. B. G. (1948).** On the attraction between two perfectly conducting plates. *Proc. K. Ned. Akad. Wet.* 51, 793–795 (communicated at the meeting of 29 May 1948; Natuurkundig Laboratorium der N.V. Philips' Gloeilampenfabrieken, Eindhoven). No DOI. KNAW reprint in *Gems from a century of science 1898–1997* (1997), pp. 61–63: <https://www.ph.nat.tum.de/fileadmin/w00bya/t75/pdf/Casimir1948.pdf>. — *Access:* full text read (note 02; scanned page images of the KNAW reprint read directly). — Derives the attractive, material-independent force between ideal conducting plates, with energy per unit area −π²ħc/(720a³) and force per unit area π²ħc/(240a⁴), about 0.013 dyn/cm² at 1 µm, valid while the separation greatly exceeds the penetration depth; the force "may be interpreted as a zero point pressure of electromagnetic waves". Casimir gives only the energy per area and the force; the uniform energy density −π²ħc/(720a⁴) follows by dividing by a, which is note 02's step.
- **Brown, L. S. & Maclay, G. J. (1969).** Vacuum stress between conducting plates: an image solution. *Physical Review* 184, 1272–1279 (published 25 August 1969 per Crossref). <https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.184.1272>. ## Topology change, causality and chronology protection

- **Geroch, R. P. (1967).** Topology in general relativity. *Journal of Mathematical Physics* 8(4), 782–786. <https://doi.org/10.1063/1.1705276>. - **Fu, Z., Grado-White, B. & Marolf, D. Note: the authors point out that rigorous bounds on wormhole transit times are hard to prove in 4D asymptotically flat spacetime, so "never faster than outside" is well supported but not a general 4D theorem (note 02).
- **Maldacena, J. & Milekhin, A. (2021).** Humanly traversable wormholes. *Physical Review D* 103, 066007. <https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.103.066007>. arXiv:2008.06618 (v2, 3 November 2020), <https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.06618>. — *Access:* full text read (arXiv v2, complete) (note 02). — Explicitly "science fiction": a hidden dark sector (a Randall–Sundrum II construction) could support a wormhole with tides a person could survive, with a throat radius of about 1.5×10^7 m (about 0.05 light-seconds), crossed in about 0.16 s of the traveller's time but about 10^4 years as seen from outside, so it is never faster than travelling through ordinary space. Infalling radiation would be blueshifted by about 10^12, so the wormhole would have to be kept extremely cold and clean, and "We have not given any plausible mechanism for their formation".
- **Maldacena, J., Milekhin, A. & Popov, F. (2023).** Traversable wormholes in four dimensions. *Classical and Quantum Gravity* 40, 155016. <https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6382/acde30> (INSPIRE). arXiv:1807.04726 (v3, 3 November 2020), <https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.04726>. — *Access:* full text read (note 02: arXiv v3, Secs. 1, 2, 4, 5, 6.1, 7, 8, 9; note 03: §§1, 5.5, 8, 9). — A controlled 4D construction: two oppositely magnetically charged near-extremal black holes whose throats join, with charged massless fermions moving along the closed magnetic field lines supplying Casimir-like negative energy; the ANEC-violating lines are not achronal, so no theorem is broken, but "it takes longer to go through the wormhole than through the ambient space". Embedded in the Standard Model it needs mouth separations below the electroweak length, so it is microscopic, it is fragile ("not safe for human travelers"), and no formation procedure is given. Note: its claim that no exotic matter is needed means no non-standard fields, not no NEC violation (note 02).
- **Jafferis, D., Zlokapa, A., Lykken, J. D., Kolchmeyer, D. K., Davis, S. I., Lauk, N., Neven, H. & Spiropulu, M. (2022).** Traversable wormhole dynamics on a quantum processor. *Nature* 612, 51–55 (issue 7938; published online 30 November 2022). <https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-022-05424-3>. No arXiv version (INSPIRE). Supplementary Information from CaltechAUTHORS record 35vq2-3j836. — *Access:* note 03: main text abstract only (the Nature page metadata says isAccessibleForFree: false), Supplementary Information full text read (33 pp.); note 04: abstract only (RePEc). — Ran a machine-learned, sparsified SYK model "with 164 two-qubit gates on a nine-qubit circuit" on Google's Sycamore processor and interpreted the teleportation signal, through holographic duality, as traversable-wormhole dynamics in a dual 2D gravity description. The protocol equals one Trotter step of the Maldacena–Qi coupled system, and with the left–right coupling switched off no information passes, so the direct coupling is essential; nothing moves through space. Note: the Supplementary Information is internally inconsistent (a sparse N = 7 model in §V.A but "N = 5 with 5 terms" in §IV); the main text's wording could not be checked; a 2025 Author Correction changed the Fig. 3b caption; for the critique see Kobrin et al. 2023.
- **Caltech (2022).** Press release on the quantum-processor wormhole experiment, 30 November 2022. — *Access:* read in full, with quotes verified against the raw HTML (note 03); note 04 also used "the Caltech news release". No URL recorded. — States that "The experiment has not created an actual wormhole (a rupture in space and time)"; Spiropulu adds that it does not substitute for direct probes of quantum gravity and that with 50% higher error rates the signal would have been obscured, and Preskill calls it "a reasonable caricature" of what the gravitational picture predicts.

- **Kobrin, B., Schuster, T. & Yao, N. Y. (2023).** Comment on "Traversable wormhole dynamics on a quantum processor". arXiv:2302.07897 (15 February 2023); not journal-published per INSPIRE. — *Access:* note 03: full text read; note 04: abstract read. — Argues that the learned Hamiltonian "does not exhibit thermalization" (individual correlators revive, and the decay in the original Fig. 3b is an artifact of averaging), that the teleportation signal looks SYK-like only for the operators the model was trained on, and that "perfect size winding" appears generically in small commuting models; none of the authors' models both size-winds and thermalises.
- **Jafferis et al. (2023).** Comment on "Comment on 'Traversable wormhole dynamics on a quantum processor'". arXiv:2303.15423 (27 March 2023); CaltechAUTHORS:20230316-190343094; FERMILAB-PUB-23-120-ETD. — *Access:* full text read (§§I–IV) (note 03). — The original authors reply that the objections "concern counterfactual scenarios outside of the experimentally implemented protocol": the coupled Hamiltonian does thermalise, all fermions size-wind but at different times, and similar models behaving similarly is "unsurprising"; they concede that a non-bulk explanation always exists. Note: they count 36 operators for an N = 7 SYK model where Kobrin et al. count 64, and note 03 leaves the discrepancy unresolved.
- **Brown, Gharibyan, Leichenauer, Lin, Nezami, Salton, Susskind, Swingle & Walter (2023).** Quantum Gravity in the Lab. I: Teleportation by Size and Traversable Wormholes. *PRX Quantum* 4, 010320. <https://doi.org/10.1103/PRXQuantum.4.010320>. arXiv:1911.06314. — *Access:* abstract plus introduction read (full text downloaded; §I read) (note 03). — Shows that teleportation protocols with entangled thermofield-double states, scrambling and a weak coupling between the halves reproduce traversable-wormhole behaviour through "teleportation by size", and proposes Rydberg-atom and trapped-ion experiments. Note: the abstract cautions that a form of signalling also works in generic chaotic systems at high temperature and late times, and that this "does not correspond to a signal going through a geometrical wormhole".

## Laboratory analogues and related experiments

- **Pendry, J. B., Schurig, D. & Smith, D. R. (2006).** Controlling electromagnetic fields. *Science* 312, 1780–1782. <https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1125907>. Author-hosted copy: <https://my.ece.utah.edu/~dschurig/Site/Recognition_files/1780.pdf>. — *Access:* full text read (scan of the published article) (note 04). — The transformation-optics recipe: a coordinate transformation is mapped onto a material's permittivity and permeability so that electromagnetic fields can be "redirected at will", illustrated by a spherical cloak; it is the general method behind any metamaterial "portal" or "wormhole". Its hard limit is that cloaking parameters must vary with frequency, so a design works fully at only one frequency (unless embedded in a high-index medium), and nothing about spacetime changes.
- **Leonhardt, U. (2006).** Optical conformal mapping. *Science* 312, 1777–1780. Preprint title: "Optical Conformal Mapping and Dielectric Invisibility Devices". <https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1126493>. arXiv:physics/0602092. — *Access:* abstract only (arXiv), plus the final page of the published article, which is in the same PDF as Pendry et al. at the Utah URL; the body was not read (note 04). — Ideal invisibility is impossible because light is a wave, but a device can be perfect within geometrical optics: "One can never completely hide from waves, but can from rays." For an optical "invisible tunnel" this means some reflection and delay always remain, although they can be made very small for objects much larger than the wavelength.
- **Schurig, D., Mock, J. J., Justice, B. J., Cummer, S. A., Pendry, J. B., Starr, A. F. & Smith, D. R. (2006).** Metamaterial electromagnetic cloak at microwave frequencies. *Science* 314, 977–980. <https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1133628>. Copy: <https://my.ece.utah.edu/~dschurig/Site/Top_Cited_Publications_files/977.pdf>. — *Access:* full text read (scan of the published article) (note 04). — The first built metamaterial cloak: two-dimensional, made of ten concentric cylinders of split-ring resonators (inner radius 27.1 mm, outer radius 58.9 mm) around a copper cylinder, working at 8.5 GHz over a narrow band and reducing, but not eliminating, backscatter and shadow. Note 04 treats it as roughly the state of the art for physically approximating designs such as Greenleaf et al.'s: centimetre-scale, 2D, one microwave band, imperfect.
- **Greenleaf, A., Kurylev, Y., Lassas, M. & Uhlmann, G. (2007).** Electromagnetic wormholes and virtual magnetic monopoles from metamaterials. *Physical Review Letters* 99, 183901 (the arXiv title omits "from metamaterials"). <https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.99.183901>. arXiv:math-ph/0703059 (v1, 20 March 2007). — *Access:* full text read (arXiv v1, 4 pp.; the published PRL version was not accessed and may differ slightly) (note 04). — A purely theoretical design, never built, for a metamaterial coating whose singular permittivity and permeability make electromagnetic waves at one design frequency travel as if space had a handle attached: an invisible optical tunnel that works as a wormhole only "with respect to Maxwell's equations". It is narrow-band, the device has an ordinary physical body (a solid cylinder with flared ends), and it involves no spacetime curvature and no transport of matter.
- **Prat-Camps, J., Navau, C. & Sanchez, A. (2015).** A magnetic wormhole. *Sci. Rep.* 5, 12488 (published 20 August 2015; Universitat Autònoma de Barcelona). <https://doi.org/10.1038/srep12488>. PMC4542659: <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4542659>. Supplementary Information: <https://static-content.springer.com/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fsrep12488/MediaObjects/41598_2015_BFsrep12488_MOESM1_ESM.pdf>. — *Access:* full text read (PMC main text and the Supplementary Information PDF with the Methods) (note 04). — Built and tested a magnetostatic "wormhole": an 87 mm mu-metal spiral tube inside a superconducting shell (run in liquid nitrogen) and a ferromagnetic metasurface, roughly 86 mm across by note 04's arithmetic, which carries a static magnetic field from one end to the other, where it emerges looking like an isolated monopole, while the device as a whole barely disturbs an applied field. It was confirmed only for DC fields and is framed as a magnetic illusion with possible MRI uses; no light, matter or spacetime connection passes through.
- **Zhu, J., Liu, Y., Liang, Z., Chen, T. & Li, J. (2018).** Elastic waves in curved space: mimicking a wormhole. *Physical Review Letters* 121, 234301. <https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.121.234301>. — *Access:* abstract only (HKUST research portal; no arXiv preprint found) (note 04). — The abstract describes a two-dimensional analogue of a wormhole for flexural waves, using homogeneous materials on a geometrically curved plate in place of extreme refractive indices. Note: what was physically built or measured cannot be confirmed from the abstract, and note 04 says not to cite numbers from this paper.
- **Unruh, W. G. (1981).** Experimental black-hole evaporation? *Physical Review Letters* 46, 1351–1353 (published 25 May 1981). <https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.46.1351>. Scan: <https://personal.lse.ac.uk/robert49/teaching/partiii/2020-2021/pdf/Unruh1981a.pdf>. — *Access:* full text read (OCR scan) (note 04). — The founding proposal of analogue gravity: sound in a transsonic flow sees an effective metric with a sonic horizon that should emit a thermal spectrum of sound, at about 3×10^-7 K for a 300 m/s flow varying over 1 mm, which Unruh thought "probably undetectable" amid turbulence. Note: the exponent is illegible in the OCR; 3×10^-7 K matches the standard restatement and note 04's own calculation (about 3.6×10^-7 K).
- **Barceló, C., Liberati, S. & Visser, M. (2005; updated 2011 and 2024).** Analogue Gravity. *Living Rev. Relativ.* 8, 12 (2005; accepted 7 November 2005, published 16 December 2005, 434 references), <https://doi.org/10.12942/lrr-2005-12>; update *Living Rev. Relativ.* 14, 3 (2011; accepted 28 February 2011, published 11 May 2011, 702 references, new Section 6), <https://doi.org/10.12942/lrr-2011-3>, EMIS mirror <https://www.maths.tcd.ie/EMIS/journals/LRG/Articles/lrr-2011-3/>; arXiv:gr-qc/0505065, v4 (29 November 2024), described as a "Major revision, updated and expanded" of the 2011 version. — *Access:* 2011 version: landing page and abstract (EMIS mirror); 2005 version: bibliographic only; arXiv v4 (2024): full text searched and relevant passages read (note 04). — The authoritative statement that analogue systems reproduce kinematics (how fields propagate on a given effective geometry) but not dynamics: analogue Hawking experiments are "purely kinematic", and a Bose–Einstein condensate never obeys the Einstein equations, so analogues cannot test whether a wormhole can be created or held open. Its literature survey lists several analogue-wormhole papers, of which note 04 verified only Euvé & Rousseaux 2017 as an experiment. Note: the quotations are from v4 and may not appear word for word in the 2005 or 2011 versions.
- **Steinhauer, J. (2016).** Observation of quantum Hawking radiation and its entanglement in an analogue black hole. *Nature Phys.* 12, 959. <https://doi.org/10.1038/nphys3863>. arXiv:1510.00621 (v3, 8 August 2017). — *Access:* full text read (arXiv v3) (note 04). — Observed spontaneous analogue Hawking radiation from a sonic horizon in a rubidium-87 Bose–Einstein condensate, using density correlations from 4600 repetitions over six days of continuous measurement, with a Hawking temperature of 1.2 nK; high-energy partner pairs were entangled and low-energy ones were not. No gravity is involved: the horizon is for phonons in a cloud a few micrometres wide.
- **Peloquin, C., Euvé, L.-P., Philbin, T. & Rousseaux, G. (2016).** Analog wormholes and black hole laser effects in hydrodynamics. *Physical Review D* 93, 084032. <https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.93.084032>. arXiv:1512.03386. — *Access:* abstract only (arXiv and the Exeter repository, via search) (note 04). — A numerical study of water waves on a varying current with surface tension: some velocity profiles give traversable, bidirectional analogue wormholes, and negative energy can be trapped between six horizons in a "laser-like cavity".
- **Euvé, L.-P. & Rousseaux, G. (2017).** Classical analogue of an interstellar travel through a hydrodynamic wormhole. *Physical Review D* 96, 064042 (published 25 September 2017; Institut Pprime, Poitiers). <https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.96.064042>. Author copy: <https://germain-rousseaux.cnrs.fr/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/PRD2017.pdf>. — *Access:* full text read (author-hosted copy of the published article, 15 pp.) (note 04). — The only laboratory analogue-wormhole experiment note 04 could verify: in a 6.5 m water channel, long gravity waves blocked at an analogue white-hole horizon turned into short capillary waves that crossed the "tunnel" region and emerged on the other side, demonstrating "analogue traversable and bidirectional wormholes" in hydrodynamics. It is a kinematic analogy only, with micrometre ripples as the travellers and no exotic matter; the authors say their results "do not apply to putative astrophysical wormholes per se", and its stability reflects the Navier–Stokes equations, not Einstein's. Note: the analogue metric is exact only without dispersion, yet the effect relies on dispersion (note 04).
- **Sabín, C. (2018).** Light propagation through nanophotonics wormholes. *Universe* 4, 137. <https://doi.org/10.3390/universe4120137>. arXiv:1807.07869. — *Access:* abstract only (note 04). — Proposes a 3D nanophotonic structure shaped like a wormhole spacetime and predicts phase and group-velocity changes, saying it can be realised with current nanophotonics; it is a proposal with no experiment reported, and note 04 found no experimental optical analogue wormhole.
- **Muñoz de Nova, J. R., Golubkov, K., Kolobov, V. I. & Steinhauer, J. (2019).** Observation of thermal Hawking radiation and its temperature in an analogue black hole. *Nature* 569, 688–691. <https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-019-1241-0>. arXiv:1809.00913 (v2, 14 September 2018, titled "Observation of thermal Hawking radiation at the Hawking temperature in an analogue black hole"). — *Access:* full text read (arXiv v2 preprint); the published Nature version was not accessed (note 04). — In a condensate of 8000 rubidium-87 atoms over 7400 runs, the analogue Hawking spectrum was found to be thermal at the temperature predicted from the analogue surface gravity, 0.35 nK in the preprint. Note: Phys.org (May 2019) gives "0.035 billionths of a kelvin", which conflicts with the preprint and is unresolved against the Nature text; a frequently repeated "97,000 repetitions / 124 days" figure could not be verified; Physics World notes that some physicists dispute how far the experiment validates the assumptions behind Hawking's prediction.
- **Pound, R. V. & Rebka, G. A., Jr. (1960).** Apparent weight of photons. *Physical Review Letters* 4, 337–341 (1 April 1960). <https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.4.337>. — *Access:* bibliographic only (note 04; APS returned 403 and the Temple University PDF copy returned 503 or failed TLS); content via Pound 1961 and Mester's lecture notes. — The first terrestrial measurement of gravitational redshift: Mössbauer spectroscopy of the 14.4 keV gamma ray of iron-57, sent up and down the enclosed tower of Harvard's Jefferson Physical Laboratory; per Mester the 1960 result was 2Δ = (5.13 ± 0.51)×10^-15, "a result good to 10%". Note: the height differs between sources ("about 21 meters" as the height used, per Pound 1961; a 22.5 m tower, per Mester), and the often-quoted ratio of 1.05 ± 0.10 was not read in the PRL itself, although it is consistent with note 04's own arithmetic.
- **Pound, R. V. (1961).** On the weight of photons. *Sov. Phys. Usp.* 3, 875–880 (translation of *Usp. Fiz. Nauk* 72, 673 (1960)). <https://ufn.ru/ufn61/ufn61_6/ufn616f.pdf>. — *Access:* full text read (note 04). — Pound's own review of the experiment: a height of about 21 m, an expected one-way shift of about 2.5×10^-15, a predicted up–down difference of 4.67×10^-15, and an average over about six runs of 0.98 ± 0.04 of the predicted gh/c², agreement "at least within about 4 percent".
- **Pound & Snider (1964).** *Physical Review Letters* 13, 539. — *Access:* bibliographic only (note 04). — The later version of the experiment, which reached 1% accuracy (per Mester).
- **Mester, J. (2006).** Experimental Tests of General Relativity (Stanford; IHP 2006 lectures). <https://luth.obspm.fr/IHP06/lectures/mester-vinet/IHP-2GravRedshift.pdf>. — *Access:* used as secondary lecture notes; note 04 gives no access label, but its 1960 Pound–Rebka numbers come only from these notes. — Source of the 22.5 m tower height, the 1960 result with its "good to 10%" accuracy, and the later 1% Pound–Snider result.
- **Hafele, J. C. & Keating, R. E. (1972a).** Around-the-world atomic clocks: predicted relativistic time gains. *Science* 177, 166–168 (14 July 1972). <https://doi.org/10.1126/science.177.4044.166>. Scan of both papers: <http://leapsecond.com/museum/HK50/1972-Science-177-HK.pdf> (personal scan by T. Van Baak). — *Access:* full text read (OCR scan of both papers) (note 04). — Predicts the time gained by cesium clocks flown east and west around the world: a gravitational term of +144 ± 14 ns (east) and +179 ± 18 ns (west), a kinematic term of −184 ± 18 ns and +96 ± 10 ns, and net gains of −40 ± 23 ns and +275 ± 21 ns.
- **Hafele, J. C. & Keating, R. E. (1972b).** "…: observed relativistic time gains" (title as recorded in note 04, the companion to the paper above). *Science* 177, 168–170 (14 July 1972). <https://doi.org/10.1126/science.177.4044.168>. Same scan as above. — *Access:* full text read (OCR scan) (note 04). — Four cesium-beam clocks flown on commercial jets in October 1971 gained −59 ± 10 ns (eastward) and +273 ± 7 ns (westward) relative to the US Naval Observatory time scale, which the authors call an "unambiguous empirical resolution" of the clock paradox with macroscopic clocks. Note: critics have disputed the clocks' stability and the data analysis (note 04 did not evaluate those critiques), the scan's OCR reads "mrcroscopic" for "macroscopic", and modern optical-clock experiments supersede it in precision.
- **Chou, C. W., Hume, D. B., Rosenband, T. & Wineland, D. J. (2010).** Optical clocks and relativity. *Science* 329, 1630–1633 (24 September 2010). <https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1192720>. NIST-hosted copy: <https://tsapps.nist.gov/publication/get_pdf.cfm?pub_id=905055>. — *Access:* full text read (note 04). — Two aluminium-ion optical clocks in separate labs, linked by 75 m of fibre, detected the shift from raising one clock by 33 cm, a fractional frequency change of (4.1 ± 1.6)×10^-17, and also detected time dilation at speeds below 10 m/s; in the authors' illustration, a clock 1 km higher ticks about three more seconds in a million years. Note: the NIST catalogue entry wrongly labels the journal as Nature and gives the title as "Relativity and Optical Clocks"; the PDF is the Science article.
- **Takamoto, M., Ushijima, I., Ohmae, N., Yahagi, T., Kokado, K., Shinkai, H. & Katori, H. (2020).** Test of general relativity by a pair of transportable optical lattice clocks. *Nature Photon.* 14, 411–415. <https://doi.org/10.1038/s41566-020-0619-8>. — *Access:* bibliographic only for the paper itself (via a University of Tokyo news page of 9 July 2020); the numbers come from the authors' 2025 review, read in full (note 04). — Two transportable strontium optical lattice clocks on the ground and observatory floors of Tokyo Skytree, 452.596 ± 0.013 m apart, measured the gravitational redshift (gΔh/c² ≈ 4.93×10^-14) and limited any deviation from general relativity to α = (1.4 ± 9.1)×10^-5, which the review calls "the best constraint on the gravitational redshift on the ground".
- **Bothwell, T., Kennedy, C. J., Aeppli, A., Kedar, D., Robinson, J. M., Oelker, E., Staron, A. & Ye, J. (2022).** Resolving the gravitational redshift across a millimetre-scale atomic sample. *Nature* 602, 420–424. <https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-021-04349-7>. arXiv:2109.12238 (v1; the preprint is titled "…within a millimeter atomic sample"). — *Access:* full text read (arXiv v1); the published abstract (via RePEc) confirms the headline 7.6×10^-21 figure (note 04). — Resolved the gravitational redshift gradient inside a single millimetre-scale sample of about 100,000 strontium atoms: −9.8(2.3)×10^-20 per mm against a predicted −1.09×10^-19 per mm, with a fractional-frequency uncertainty of 7.6×10^-21 in a 92-hour comparison. Note 04 concludes that portal mouths at different heights, even a millimetre apart, could not stay perfectly time-synchronised.
- **Shinkai, H., Takamoto, M. & Katori, H. (2025).** Transportable optical lattice clocks and general relativity. arXiv:2502.06104 (February 2025). — *Access:* full text read (note 04). — The authors' review from which note 04 takes the Tokyo Skytree numbers (a frequency shift of about 21.18 Hz at about 429.228 THz over a fibre link of about 700 m); it also cites Galileo-satellite redshift tests that note 04 did not read.
- **Bennett, C. H., Brassard, G., Crépeau, C., Jozsa, R., Peres, A. & Wootters, W. K. (1993).** Teleporting an unknown quantum state via dual classical and Einstein-Podolsky-Rosen channels. *Physical Review Letters* 70, 1895–1899 (29 March 1993). <https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.70.1895>. Scan: <https://people.disim.univaq.it/~serva/teaching/Bennet.1993.pdf>. — *Access:* full text read (OCR scan) (note 04). — The teleportation protocol: a joint measurement on the unknown particle and on half of an entangled pair, plus two classical bits sent to the receiver, recreates the state on a particle that is already there and destroys the original; "The classical message plays an essential role in teleportation", and anything less than two bits would allow faster-than-light signalling. It moves a state, not matter, and not instantaneously.
- **Bouwmeester, D., Pan, J.-W., Mattle, K., Eibl, M., Weinfurter, H. & Zeilinger, A. (1997).** Experimental quantum teleportation. *Nature* 390, 575–579. <https://doi.org/10.1038/37539>. arXiv:1901.11004 (posted 2019, dated 11 December 1997). — *Access:* full text read (arXiv copy) (note 04). — The first photonic demonstration: a photon's polarisation state was teleported onto another photon, but only one of the four Bell states was identified, so it succeeded "only in a quarter of the cases", with visibilities of 0.57–0.66 after subtracting spurious coincidences. Note: the distance is not given in the parts read, and note 04 says not to quote one.
- **Ren, J.-G. et al., including C.-Z. Peng, J.-Y. Wang and J.-W. Pan (2017).** Ground-to-satellite quantum teleportation. *Nature* 549, 70–73. <https://doi.org/10.1038/nature23675>. arXiv:1707.00934 (v1). — *Access:* full text read (arXiv v1) (note 04). — Single-photon qubits were teleported from the Ngari ground station in Tibet to the Micius satellite over 500–1400 km, with 911 four-photon counts in 32 orbits and an average fidelity of 0.80 ± 0.01, above the classical limit of 2/3. Even this record moves no mass: the receiving photon is already on the satellite, and the result cannot be read until classical information arrives at light speed or slower.
- **Pepper, J. H. (1890).** The True History of the Ghost; and All About Metempsychosis. *Cassell, London*. Project Gutenberg eBook #72672: <https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/72672>. — *Access:* full text searched; relevant passages read (note 04). — Reproduces the Dircks & Pepper patent ("Apparatus for exhibiting dramatic and other performances", A.D. 1863 No. 326, dated 5 February 1863, sealed 25 September 1863): an inclined sheet of glass that the audience does not notice reflects a brightly lit hidden stage, so actors seem to stand where they are not, and the image vanishes when the hidden stage is darkened. This "Pepper's ghost" is the classic "doorway to elsewhere" stage illusion. Note: Pepper's account is internally inconsistent about the private view of December 1862 (it names both "The Haunted Man" and Bulwer's "A Strange Story"); secondary sources usually date the debut to 24 December 1862, so exact dates and plays are uncertain.
- **Galloway, K. & Rabinowitz, S. (1980).** Hole In Space: A Public Communication Sculpture (November 1980).
