# KESTREL S1: electrical architecture

S1 is the simple, cheap, modular build of KESTREL: 1.75 m, 65.5 kg estimated (72 kg design mass with the
10 % growth allowance), 27 joint actuators plus 2 gripper actuators, all integrated QDD modules on CAN.
This document covers power, regeneration, the e-stop, wiring, CAN, grounding, connector pinouts and how to
add components. Software is in [S1-software.md](S1-software.md); prices are in
[../generated/bom.md](../generated/bom.md) (generated by `simple/calc/s1_bom.py`).

It is deliberately much simpler than K1 ([docs/05](../../docs/05-electrical-architecture.md)): one bought
battery, one contactor, five CAN buses, no custom PCBs except two small boards (regen clamp and e-stop
logic) that fit on protoboard.

## 2. Power budget and battery sizing

### 2.1 Actuator power from the simulated scenarios

Electrical power per actuator, for every 5 ms sample of every S1 scenario [sim], using maker data:

```
tau_a, w_a   actuator-side torque/speed: joint values through the external reduction
             (hip roll 2:1 crank, knee 1.5:1 belt, eta 0.95; ankle through the K1 parallel push-rod linkage)
P_mech     = tau_a * w_a
P_copper   = 1.5 * R_LL * (tau_a / Kt)^2           (Kt in N·m/Arms at the output, R_LL line-to-line)
P_elec     = (P_mech + P_copper) * (1 + 0.03 sign) (3 % drive loss)
regeneration (P_elec < 0) is credited at 50 %, as in K1's calc/kestrel/power.py
```

### 2.3 Operating modes and runtime

| Mode | Actuators | + standby + electronics | **Total** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Standby (drives enabled, robot hanging) | 0 | 80 W | **80 W** |
| Standing | 40 W | 80 W | **120 W** |

**Battery.** 13S × 3.6 V × 20 Ah = **936 Wh nominal**. Usable energy with the 4.10 V/cell charge limit
(§3) and a 3.3 V/cell (42.9 V) cut-off, which keeps the X12 knee speed margin [E: 75 % window] and 3 %
wiring loss: 0.75 × 936 × 0.97 ≈ **680 Wh**.

| Mode | Runtime = 680 Wh / power |
|---|---|
| Mixed duty | **3.0 h** |
| Continuous walking 0.8 m/s | **2.0 h** |
| Continuous walking 1.0 m/s / stairs | **1.5 h** |
| Standby on the gantry | 8.5 h |

**Why 20 Ah and not 15 Ah.** Peak power at the W2 push-off is 2,132 + 50 + 48 ≈ 2.23 kW → **51 A at a
sagged 44 V** [sim]. A 13S4P pack (20 Ah from 5 Ah cells) carries 12.7 A per cell at that instant; a 13S3P
(15 Ah) pack would carry 17 A per cell and its BMS would typically be rated lower. 15 Ah would also give
only ≈ 1.1 h of fast walking.

**Chosen pack:** Melbourne Powered "Polly" 48 V 20 Ah, Samsung 50G 21700 cells, **50 A continuous**,
Micro XT90 discharge, 4.95 kg, 367 × 95 × 125 mm, 3 A charger, A$895.95 [S]. The S1 mass model allows
5.8 kg for a ~1 kWh pack, so it fits the budget.

- **UNVERIFIED:** the page states only "48 V 20 Ah"; confirm 13S (not 14S) with the seller before buying.
  A 14S pack (58.8 V full) would destroy the X12s.
- **Peak vs BMS rating:** 51 A is at the BMS's 50 A continuous rating for ~0.1 s. Its overcurrent trip delay is
  not published. Test it (§13) and, until then, cap total commanded power in software (S1-software §6.2).
  At 0.8 m/s the peak is 1,236 W + 98 W → 30 A, well inside the rating.
- The budget alternative in R9 (TDR 48 V 20 Ah, A$609) is limited to 40 A continuous: below S1's peak.
- **Shipping:** Australia Post does not accept lithium batteries over 100 Wh (R6, cited in K1 docs/05); buy
  for pickup or courier.

## 3. Bus voltage limits (read this first)

| Device | Limit | Source |
|---|---|---|
| MyActuator X12-320 | "Modules with an input voltage of 48 VDC have a maximum withstand voltage of 55 VDC at the power interface"; the X12-320 spec page adds "The maximum operating voltage of the motor is 55 V" although its table lists 20-70 V | [M: User Manual for X Series Products V1.1-250516 §5.1; X Series-V4 product manual 261029, X12-320 page] |
| RobStride RS00/02/03/04 | maximum allowable 60 VDC; overvoltage fault at 60 V, undervoltage at 12 V | [M: RS04 manual 260713 §2.1 and §3.3.7] |
| 13S pack | 54.6 V at 4.20 V/cell; **53.3 V at the 4.10 V/cell S1 charge limit** | arithmetic |

**Rule: the actuator bus must never exceed 55 V.** The table/note conflict for the X12 is resolved to the
stricter 55 V. With a 4.20 V/cell charge the pack rests at 54.6 V, only 0.4 V below the limit, so no
clamp threshold can sit between "full battery" and "X12 damage". Hence:

1. Charge to **53.3 V (4.10 V/cell)** with an adjustable-voltage CC/CV charger. Do not use the 54.6 V
   charger that comes with the pack. This costs some capacity ([E] included in the 75 % window above).
2. Regen clamp ON at 54.3 V, OFF at 53.9 V (§5).
3. If MyActuator confirms in writing that 70 V is the X12's real limit, the clamp can move to 57 V and the
   charge limit back to 4.20 V/cell. Until then, 55 V rules.

## 4. Power architecture

### 4.1 Power path

```
                 PACK 13S 20 Ah (BMS 50 A inside)
                 B+ ──[Micro XT90]──┬──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
                                    │                                                              │
                              F0 MAIN FUSE 80 A                                               (B- / 0 V)
                              DC >= 60 V, >= 2 kA                                                  │
                                    │                                                              │
                     ┌──────────────┴───────────── 48V-RAW ─────────────────┐                      │
                     │                                                      │                      │
              F1 7.5 A MINI58  LOGIC BRANCH (never cut by e-stop)     K2 precharge relay           │
                     │                                                  + R_pre 22 Ω 50 W          │
             KEY SWITCH (main on/off)                                       │                      │
                     │                                       ┌──── EV200 contactor (coil = e-stop  │
          ┌──────────┴───────────┐                           │      loop, §6) ───┐                 │
   DDR-120C-12 (12 V 10 A)  DDR-120C-24 (24 V 5 A)           │                   │                 │
          │                      │                           └────── 48V-SW ─────┴─ C_bus 2x1000 µF│
   12 V bus (fused taps)    24 V bus (fused taps)                      │            + TVS SMDJ58A  │
   - Jetson 5 A              - spare 24 V taps                         │            + REGEN CLAMP  │
   - USB hub 3 A                                                       │              (2.35 Ω)     │
   - e-stop loop 2 A                                       ┌───────────┴─ 48V-SW BUS BAR ───┐      │
   - Ethernet switch 1 A                                   │                                │      │
   - 12->5 V buck 3 A -> 5 V taps                   per-branch MINI 58 V fuses:           INA228   │
   - spare 12 V taps                                30 A L-hip-pitch X12   30 A R-hip-pitch X12    │
                                                    30 A L-knee X12        30 A R-knee X12         │
                                                    20 A L-leg RS04 chain  20 A R-leg RS04 chain   │
                                                    15 A L-arm             15 A R-arm              │
                                                    10 A torso/neck/grip   10 A 48V-SW spare tap   │
```

- **48V-RAW** is live whenever the pack is plugged in and F0 is intact. The key switch turns the logic
  branch on and off.
- **48V-SW** (actuator bus) is live only when the e-stop loop is armed and the contactor has closed.
- The DC-DCs are isolated (4 kV) but their 0 V outputs are bonded to the star point (§9), so there is one
  ground reference.
- The X12s get **dedicated feeds**, as MyActuator recommends "single-axis direct connection for
  high-power modules" [M: X-series user manual §6.1]. The RobStride units are daisy-chained per limb.

### 4.2 BMS

The pack's built-in BMS (50 A continuous [S]) provides cell over/under-voltage, over-current,
short-circuit and temperature cut-off. It is the last line of defence and is not controlled by S1. S1 adds:

- an **INA228-class power monitor** (85 V, I²C) on a 100 A / 75 mV shunt in the 48V-RAW path, read by the
  Jetson for bus voltage, current and energy (state of charge by coulomb counting) [E];
- software limits: warn at 44 V, controlled sit-down at 43 V, power off at 42 V under load (≈ 3.3 V/cell).

### 4.3 Main fuse

- **Rating: 80 A**, DC voltage rating **≥ 60 V**, breaking capacity ≥ 2 kA, as close to the pack terminal
  as possible.
- 80 A clears the 51 A peak with margin and protects the 8 AWG main conductor.
- Prospective short-circuit current [E]: ≈ 54.6 V / (pack ~80 mΩ + wiring ~10 mΩ) ≈ 0.6 kA. The cell DCIR is
  assumed, not sourced; the BMS should trip first.
- **Automotive ANL/MIDI/MEGA fuses are usually rated 32 V DC: do not use them.** Use a fuse whose DC rating
  is printed ≥ 58 V (e.g. Class T 125 VDC with holder, or a 58 V-rated part). Price is an estimate in the BOM.

### 4.4 Precharge

The actuators' input capacitance is not published. **Assume ≤ 20 mF** in total [E] (29 drives plus the
2 × 1000 µF added on the bus). Closing the contactor straight onto that would draw kiloamps for
microseconds and weld contacts. MyActuator itself warns that a switched supply overshoots at power-up and
recommends a 1000 µF/100 V capacitor after the switch [M: X-series user manual §5.1].

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| R_pre | 22 Ω, 50 W aluminium-housed |
| Time constant τ = R·C | 22 Ω × 20 mF = 0.44 s |
| Delay before the contactor closes | 3 s = 6.8 τ → bus within 0.1 % of the pack |
| Peak precharge current | 53.3 V / 22 Ω = 2.4 A |
| Energy in R_pre per precharge | ½·C·V² = ½ × 0.020 × 53.3² ≈ 28 J |
| Peak power in R_pre | 53.3² / 22 = 129 W, decaying with τ (pulse; check the resistor's overload rating) |

Sequence (hard-wired, §6): when the loop is armed (K1 latched), K2 closes immediately and charges the bus
through R_pre; a 3 s delay-on timer (T1) then energises the EV200. Software checks
`V(48V-SW) ≥ 0.95 × V(pack)` from the INA228 before enabling any actuator; if it is not met, it opens the
loop through the watchdog relay K3.

### 4.5 Contactor

**TE Kilovac EV200AAANA**: 500 A, 9-36 VDC coil, A$327.50 ex GST [S: RS AU 690-0814; R9]. It is oversized
for 51 A, but it is a sealed DC contactor designed to break DC loads and is sold in Australia. The coil is
fed from the e-stop loop at 12 V. This variant has no auxiliary contact; the contactor state is sensed from
the 48V-SW voltage.

## 5. Regeneration protection

### 5.1 Why it is needed

1. **The manuals say so.** RobStride's MIT-mode damping demo: "the motor generates electricity under this
   condition and requires power supply to prevent overvoltage" [M: RS04 manual §4.3.1]. MyActuator: with a
   battery "the back EMF of the module directly charges the battery" [M: X-series user manual §5.1]. That is
   true only while the battery is connected and willing to accept charge.
2. **After an e-stop the battery is disconnected.** The contactor isolates 48V-SW, the robot falls into the
   harness, and back-driven motors pump energy into the bus capacitance alone.
   ½·C·(55² − 53.3²) = ½ × 0.020 × 184 ≈ **1.8 J** of headroom. A falling robot has hundreds of joules of
   potential energy. Without a clamp the bus rises past 55 V.
3. **Rectified back-EMF alone can exceed 55 V** (no drive action needed):
   - X12: 17.9 V/krpm (DC) [M: X12 page] → 55 V at 3,073 motor rpm = 154 output rpm = 16.1 rad/s. That is
     10.7 rad/s at the knee through the 1.5:1 belt. The knee already reaches 6.3 rad/s in normal walking [sim],
     so a collapsing knee can exceed it.
   - RS04: 16.9 Vrms/krpm [M: RS04 manual §1.3] → peak line voltage √2 × 16.9 × 2.30 = 55 V at 2,300 motor
     rpm = 26.8 rad/s at the output. Less likely, and RobStride adds damping when unpowered ("post-power-off
     anti-backdrive protection", RS04 manual §4.2.5).
4. **The BMS can refuse charge** (cold pack, cell at its OV limit). Then even with the contactor closed, regen
   has nowhere to go.

### 5.2 Design

```
 48V-SW ──┬───────────────┬──────────────┬─────────────────────┐
          │               │              │                     │
     2x1000 µF 100 V   TVS SMDJ58A    R_brake 2.35 Ω         R1/R2 divider (0.1 %)
     low-ESR            (fast edges)  (2 x 4.7 Ω 200 W        │
          │               │            in parallel, on frame) LM393 comparator, ref TL431 (0.5 %)
          │               │              │                     hysteresis: ON 54.3 V, OFF 53.9 V
          │               │           Q1 100 V MOSFET ◄────────┘ gate drive from a 12 V regulator
          │               │           (e.g. IRFB4110 class)       fed from 48V-SW itself
 0 V ─────┴───────────────┴──────────────┴──────────────────────────────────────────────────────
```

| Parameter | Value | Arithmetic |
|---|---|---|
| ON threshold | 54.3 V | ±0.35 V tolerance (TL431 0.5 % + divider) → 53.95-54.65 V: always above the 53.3 V rest maximum and below 55 V |
| OFF threshold | 53.9 V | 0.4 V hysteresis stops chatter |
| Resistance | 2.35 Ω | at 54.3 V: I = 23.1 A, P = 54.3² / 2.35 = **1.25 kW** absorbed while on |
| Largest simulated regen | 959 W (SD missed step, knee braking), 40 J per event; DS deep squat 179 W, 104 J | 1.25 kW > 959 W, so the bus holds at the threshold |
| Fall after e-stop | ≤ 72 kg × 9.81 × 0.4 m ≈ 280 J if all the drop to a kneel came back as electricity [E: upper bound] | resistor pulse energy, check its datasheet |
| MOSFET | 100 V, ≥ 60 A, R_DS(on) ≤ 5 mΩ | 23 A² × 5 mΩ ≈ 2.7 W conduction, small heatsink |
| TVS | SMDJ58A (58 V stand-off) | stand-off above 54.6 V; its clamping voltage is far above 60 V, so it only catches nanosecond spikes. The active clamp does the real work. |

- The clamp sits on **48V-SW, downstream of the contactor**, so it protects the actuators after an e-stop.
- It powers itself from 48V-SW and works with the logic rails off.
- The resistors bolt to the aluminium torso frame as a heatsink, away from the battery.
- The INA228 logs every clamp event (bus voltage above 54 V) so clamp activity is visible.

### 5.3 Off-the-shelf clamps considered and rejected

| Product | Problem for S1 |
|---|---|
| RobStride Discharge Module | 48 V mode starts discharging at **64 ± 0.5 V**, stops at 59 ± 0.5 V [M: Discharge Module User Manual V1.0]: above both 60 V (RobStride) and 55 V (X12) |
| RobStride Power Board | discharge enable = programmed maximum voltage + 6 V, disable = maximum + 2 V; over-voltage trip 65 V [M: Power supply board instructions V1.3]. Setting ON = 54.3 V puts OFF at 50.3 V, below the resting pack, so it would drain the battery through the resistor |
| ODrive Regen Clamp (US$89) [S: shop.odriverobotics.com] | detects regeneration rather than a voltage threshold; its maximum voltage and trip point were not checked against 55 V. **UNVERIFIED** candidate: read its datasheet before using it |

### 6.2 Loop schematic (hard-wired series loop with manual reset)

```
 +12V LOGIC ─ F 2 A ─[ROBOT ESTOP ch1 NC]─[PENDANT ch1 NC]─[WIRELESS RX stop relay]─[K3 watchdog NO]─┬─ +12V ARMED
   (pendant unplugged: yellow bypass plug, tethered to the robot)                                   │
                                                                                                     │
 +12V ARMED ──┬──[RESET momentary NO]──┬── K1 coil ──┐                                               │
              └──[K1 aux NO, self-hold]┘             │                                               │
              ├── K1 main NO ──┬── K2 coil (precharge relay, closes at once)                         │
              │                └── T1 delay-on 3 s ── EV200 coil (+) ─┐                              │
              └── opto ──► Jetson GPIO "ARMED"                        │                              │
                                                                      │                              │
 0 V ◄──[ROBOT ESTOP ch2 NC]◄──[PENDANT ch2 NC]◄──────────────────────┴── coil returns of K1, K2, EV200
```

How it behaves:

- **Every stop device interrupts the coil current directly.** Pressing any button removes +12V ARMED,
  so K1, K2, T1 and the EV200 drop together regardless of relay state. A welded K1 cannot keep the bus live.
- **Dual channel on the mushrooms.** Channel 1 breaks the supply side, channel 2 the return side, so a
  single short across one contact block still leaves a working stop. The wireless relay and K3 are single
  channel.
- **Manual reset.** After an e-stop is released nothing restarts. K1 re-latches only when RESET is pressed
  with every stop device closed, the wireless link healthy and K3 energised. This follows the EN ISO 13850
  rule that resetting a stop must not by itself restart the machine.
- **Software can open the loop but never close it.** K3 is held in by a retriggerable monostable
  (74HC123) that the real-time control loop toggles at ≥ 50 Hz while it is healthy and wants the bus armed.
  If the loop stalls for more than 200 ms, or software requests a stop, K3 drops and the contactor opens.
- **What the stop does.** It removes actuator power: a stop category 0 in IEC 60204-1 terms. Every actuator
  goes limp and **the robot falls**. Hence the rule (R8, Asimov's lesson): a gantry or harness until walking
  is validated. Software stops (DAMP first, then power-off) are in S1-software §6.
- Logic and compute stay powered: logs, telemetry and the cause of the stop survive.

### 6.3 Loop currents and contact ratings

- Holding current ≈ K1 + K2 + EV200 (economised) coils, < 0.5 A [E]. The EV200's pickup current is higher for
  a fraction of a second: check its datasheet. Every contact in the loop (mushroom blocks, wireless relay, K3,
  RESET) must be rated ≥ 5 A at 12 VDC.
- Loop wiring: 18 AWG, fused 2 A at the 12 V tap.

### 6.4 Response time

The vendors do not publish total stop times (K1 R2 noted this for FORT). **Measure** from the button contact
to 48V-SW current reaching zero with a scope, for every device. Target ≤ 50 ms for the wired buttons
(contactor opening time dominates) and record the wireless link-loss timeout (§13).

## 7. CAN buses

### 7.1 Topology, node lists and IDs

Five classic CAN 2.0 buses at **1 Mbps**, the rate both makers specify [M: RS04 manual §2.1; MyActuator CAN
protocol V4.4 §1]. Daisy-chained along each limb. IDs follow R8 §5.5 (limb digit, joint digit), assigned on
the bench **before assembly**.

|---|---|---|---|
| `can_lleg` | L leg | hip yaw RS04 (11), hip roll RS04 (12), **hip pitch X12 (13)**, **knee X12 (14)**, ankle A RS04 (15), ankle B RS04 (16), L foot board (17, 100 Hz) | RobStride private protocol, 29-bit extended IDs; MyActuator standard IDs 0x140+ID (commands), 0x240+ID (replies), motion mode 0x400+ID → reply 0x500+ID |

| `can_torso` | torso + neck + grippers | waist yaw RS04 (51), neck yaw RS00 (52), neck pitch RS00 (53), L gripper RS00 (54), R gripper RS00 (55); spare drop for future nodes | RobStride private |

ID constraints from the manuals:

- MyActuator IDs are **1-32** [M: CAN protocol V4.4 §1]. The four X12s use 13, 14, 23 and 24, which fit.
- RobStride IDs are 0-127 (parameter CAN_ID). The host ID is CAN_MASTER, default 253 (0xFD) [M: RS04 manual
  §3.3.4 parameter table].
- **Keep RobStride on its 29-bit private protocol on the leg buses.** Its alternative 11-bit "MIT protocol"
  uses standard frames that could collide with MyActuator's 0x140/0x240/0x400/0x500 + ID ranges. Extended and
  standard frames never collide (the IDE bit differs).

### 7.2 Termination and wiring

- 120 Ω at **both ends** of every bus: the interface end (software-selectable on the mjcanfd-usb-1x [S:
  mjbots]) and a terminator plug in the spare drop at the far end of the limb (R8 §5.5).
- The X12 ships with a separate 120 Ω terminator [S: rcdrone.top listing], so it has no internal
  termination. RobStride does not document internal termination. **Check:** with power off, CAN_H-CAN_L
  resistance must read ≈ 60 Ω. 40 Ω means a third terminator somewhere.
- Twisted pair throughout, stubs ≤ 0.3 m (the distribution boards keep them short), bus length ≤ 3 m per limb.
- CAN ground: the RobStride CAN connectors have no ground pin. The reference is the power ground in the same
  harness, which the actuators share with the interface via the star point (§9).

### 7.3 Bus load at 500 Hz MIT-mode exchange

Each node gets one command frame and returns one reply frame per cycle. All are 8-byte data frames.

| Frame | Bits, no stuffing | Bits, worst-case stuffing |
|---|---|---|
| Standard 11-bit ID (MyActuator) | 1 SOF + 11 ID + RTR + IDE + r0 + 4 DLC + 64 data + 15 CRC + CRC delim + 2 ACK + 7 EOF + 3 IFS = **111** | + ⌊(98 − 1)/4⌋ = 24 → **135** |
| Extended 29-bit ID (RobStride) | 1 + 11 + SRR + IDE + 18 + RTR + r1 + r0 + 4 + 64 + 15 + 1 + 2 + 7 + 3 = **131** | + ⌊(118 − 1)/4⌋ = 29 → **160** |

Load = (bits per cycle × cycle rate) / 1,000,000:

| Bus | Bits per cycle (nominal / worst) | **500 Hz** | 250 Hz |
|---|---|---|---|
| Leg: 4 RobStride + 2 X12 + foot board at 100 Hz | 2 × (4 × 131 + 2 × 111) = 1,492 / 2 × (4 × 160 + 2 × 135) = 1,820; foot board 100 × 131 = 13.1 / 100 × 160 = 16.0 kbit/s | **75.9 % / 92.6 %** | 38.6 % / 47.1 % |
| Arm: 6 RobStride | 2 × 6 × 131 = 1,572 / 2 × 6 × 160 = 1,920 | **78.6 % / 96.0 %** | 39.3 % / 48.0 % |
| Torso: 5 RobStride | 2 × 5 × 131 = 1,310 / 1,600 | **65.5 % / 80.0 %** | 32.8 % / 40.0 % |

**Result: 500 Hz does not fit on five classic-CAN buses.** Every bus exceeds the S1 design ceiling of **60 %
worst-case load**, which leaves room for error frames, retransmission and added nodes. The design rate is
therefore **250 Hz** (≤ 48 % worst case). That matches R8's finding and BHL's 250 Hz, and is ample: the RL
policy runs at 50 Hz (5 CAN cycles per policy step) and the PD loop of MIT mode runs inside every actuator.

To get 500 Hz later:

1. **Split each leg into two buses** (hip yaw/roll/pitch; knee/ankle A/ankle B): 2 × (2 × 131 + 111) × 500 =
   373 kbit/s = **37 %** nominal, 46 % worst case. That is 7 buses: the spare interface plus one more
   mjcanfd-usb-1x. Arms stay at 250 Hz.
2. Or move to CAN-FD actuators. RobStride sells CAN-FD versions of the RS series [M: Product_Information
   repo, "CANFD" manuals], but the RS04-CANFD manual still states 1 Mbps and 8-byte frames, so the FD gain is
   **UNVERIFIED**. The X12 offers EtherCAT instead.

### 7.4 USB-CAN-FD interfaces

**mjbots mjcanfd-usb-1x**, US$39 each, **6 units** (5 buses + 1 spare) [S: mjbots]:

- CAN-FD (ISO 11898-1:2015), up to 12 Mbit/s data phase;
- appears as a **gs_usb SocketCAN device** under Linux (no vendor driver);
- software-selectable termination;
- ±58 V common-mode range on the CAN lines;
- **ground not isolated from USB**, which the grounding rules in §9 account for;
- CAN connector JST PH3: make a PH3-to-bus pigtail per interface (pin order: **UNVERIFIED**, check the mjbots
  reference before crimping);
- open-source STM32G4 firmware.

The interfaces run classic CAN at 1 Mbps today, and they are FD-ready for the upgrade in §7.3. Pin each
interface to a fixed Linux name with a udev rule on its USB serial number (S1-software §3); USB enumeration
order is not stable. Cheaper alternatives (R9): CANable 2.0 US$35 (FD only with slcan firmware), ODrive
USB-CAN US$37 (CAN 2.0 only). RobStride's own USB-CAN adapter (A$33.95, Pakronics) is kept for its
configuration GUI only.

## 9. Grounding and shielding

1. **One star point.** Battery negative goes to a copper bus bar on the torso electrical plate. Every 0 V
   connects there: the 48V-SW return bus bar, the DC-DC input and **output** 0 V, the e-stop loop 0 V and the
   chassis bond. The DC-DCs are isolated, but their outputs are bonded here deliberately. Otherwise the
   Jetson's ground would float and find a path to the battery only through the USB-CAN adapters' CAN grounds.
2. **Chassis bond.** The aluminium frame is bonded to the star point with one short 10 mm² strap. Anodised
   parts are insulators, so mask the contact pads. Actuator housings are bonded through their mounting flanges.
   The RobStride stator is hipot-tested to the housing (600 VAC [M: RS04 manual §1.3]), but whether the housing
   connects to the drive ground internally is not documented. **Measure** housing-to-GND resistance on the bench.
3. **CAN reference.** The mjcanfd-usb-1x is not isolated, so the interface ground is the Jetson ground =
   star point. Each actuator's CAN reference is its power ground. Ground offsets are only the power-return drops
   (≤ 0.3 V at peak, §8.1), far inside the transceivers' common-mode range (±58 V on the interfaces).
   Isolated interfaces (e.g. PEAK PCAN-USB FD, €278 [R9]) are the upgrade if CAN errors correlate with motor
   current.
4. **Shielding.** The drives are inside the actuators, so there are no long phase leads (the main EMI source
   on K1). Keep CAN pairs twisted and away from the 48 V trunks where possible. Use shielded USB 3 cables with
   ferrites for the cameras. Shields go to ground at the computer end only.
5. **IMU placement.** Mount the IMU on the pelvis at the MJCF `imu` site, on a rigid plate, ≥ 100 mm from the
   contactor, brake resistors and the battery cables (magnetometer and vibration).
6. **Brake resistors** are hot during clamp events: mount them on the frame, away from the battery and the
   harness, with a finger guard.

## 12. How to add a new component

### 12.1 Power taps (all fused, all on the torso DIN rail)

| Tap | Voltage | Fuse | Connector | Survives e-stop? | Budget left for add-ons |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 48V-SW spare | 48 V (42.9-53.3 V) | 10 A MINI 58 V | XT60 | **no** (actuator bus) | 10 A; also ≤ 60 % CAN load (§12.2) |
| 24 V | 24 V from DDR-120C-24 | 3 A each, 2 taps | XT30 2-pin, yellow | yes | 120 W converter, < 5 W used |
| 12 V | 12 V from DDR-120C-12 | 3 A each, 3 taps | 5.5 × 2.1 barrel | yes | 120 W converter, ≈ 35 W used |
| 5 V | 5 V from the 12 V buck | 2 A each, 2 taps | USB-A | yes | 25 W buck, ≈ 2 W used |

Rules:

- **New actuator:** 48V-SW (it must be cut by the e-stop).
- **New sensor or computer:** 24/12/5 V (it should survive an e-stop and keep logging).
- **Anything that can move or heat:** must be on 48V-SW or switched by software from a rail that the e-stop
  removes.
- Add its typical and peak power to §2.2 or §2.3. The logic branch fuse (7.5 A) allows ≈ 290 W of DC-DC input.

### 12.2 Spare CAN drops

- Every bus ends in a spare drop at the far end of the limb (and the torso), carrying the 120 Ω terminator.
  JST GH 4-pin, DroneCAN layout. To add a node: unplug the terminator, plug in the node, put the terminator on
  the node's output (or enable the node's own termination).
- **Budget.** At 250 Hz each additional actuator adds 2 × 131 × 250 = 65.5 kbit/s = **6.6 %**. A sensor
  reporting at 100 Hz adds 1.3 %. Leg and arm buses have room for **one** more actuator each under the 60 %
  worst-case ceiling; the torso bus has room for two. Beyond that, use the spare interface (sixth bus).
- **IDs:** pick from the limb's free range (x7-x9; torso 56-59). Never reuse an ID. MyActuator units only accept
  1-32. Write the ID on the housing.

### 12.3 USB and Ethernet

- USB 3 hub: 7 ports, 6 used by the CAN interfaces (one is the spare); one free port on the Jetson.
  The cameras sit on the Jetson's own ports.
- Ethernet switch: 5 ports, 1 to the Jetson, **3 spare** (e.g. a LiDAR, a second computer).
- Jetson 40-pin header: I²C (INA228), GPIO (ARMED input, K3 watchdog output), plus the Jetson's built-in CAN
  controller (needs an external transceiver) as a possible sixth bus.

### 12.4 Mounting on the 20 mm grid

- Every S1 plate carries **M5 clearance holes on a 20 mm pitch** that matches the 20-series extrusion slots
  (R8 §5.5). A bracket bolts to extrusion (M5 T-nuts in the 6 mm slot), to a plate or to another bracket
  without redesign.
- Boxes (computers, cameras) use VESA MIS-D 75 × 75 / 100 × 100 with M4 (R8 §5.5).
- Tube-clamp collars on the Ø40 limb tubes take sensors and P-clips anywhere along a limb.
- Electrical parts go on the 35 mm DIN rail on the torso back plate: a new fuse holder or converter clips on.

### 12.5 Checklist for any addition

1. Power: which tap, typical/peak current, fuse ≤ wire rating.
2. CAN: ID, bus, load still ≤ 60 % worst case.
3. Mass: weigh the part with its bracket and add one line to `simple/data/add_ons.json` (build guide step 11),
   then re-run `python simple/calc/s1_select.py` and `python simple/calc/s1_bom.py`. The tightest classes are the
   elbow (lowest margin 1.03) and the knee/hip-pitch X12 (1.07) (`simple/generated/actuator_selection.md`). The
   spare budget is about 4 kg on the torso or head and 0.3 kg per hand (build guide step 11).
4. Safety: if it can move, it must stop when the e-stop opens and respect the software limits.
5. Update this document's tables (power, IDs).

## 15. Appendix: reproducing the power numbers

Run this with the project venv from `projects/kestrel-humanoid` (it reads only committed data):

```python
import json, numpy as np
z = np.load("simple/data/scenario_series.npz")
names = json.load(open("simple/data/scenario_meta.json"))["joints"]
MOD = {"RS04": (2.1, 0.16), "RS03": (2.36, 0.39), "RS02": (1.22, 0.55), "RS00": (1.48, 1.5), "X12": (3.3, 0.12)}
JM = {"hip_yaw": ("RS04", 1), "hip_roll": ("RS04", 2), "hip_pitch": ("X12", 1), "knee": ("X12", 1.5),
      "waist_yaw": ("RS04", 1), "shoulder_pitch": ("RS04", 1), "shoulder_roll": ("RS04", 1),
      "shoulder_yaw": ("RS03", 1), "elbow": ("RS03", 1), "wrist_yaw": ("RS02", 1), "wrist_pitch": ("RS02", 1),
      "neck_yaw": ("RS00", 1), "neck_pitch": ("RS00", 1)}
def pe(t, w, m):                       # signed electrical power of one actuator
    kt, R = MOD[m]; p = t * w + 1.5 * R * (t / kt) ** 2; return p + 0.03 * np.abs(p)
def scen(sc):
    tau, qd, tot = z[f"{sc}/tau"], z[f"{sc}/qd"], 0
    for i, n in enumerate(names):
        b = n[2:] if n[:2] in ("l_", "r_") else n
        if b.startswith("ankle"): continue
        m, r = JM[b]; t, w = tau[:, i], qd[:, i]
        if r != 1: t = np.where(t * w >= 0, t / (r * .95), t * .95 / r); w = w * r
        tot = tot + pe(t, w, m)
    for s in "lr":                     # parallel ankle: crank 40 mm, foot lever 52 mm, half spacing 36 mm
        p, ro = tau[:, names.index(f"{s}_ankle_pitch")], tau[:, names.index(f"{s}_ankle_roll")]
        wp, wr = qd[:, names.index(f"{s}_ankle_pitch")], qd[:, names.index(f"{s}_ankle_roll")]
        for g in (1, -1):
            F = p / .104 + g * ro / .072; v = wp * .052 + g * wr * .036
            tot = tot + pe(F * .04, v / .04, "RS04")
    return tot
for sc in ("W1", "W2", "ST", "SQ", "AH", "SD"):
    t = scen(sc); m = np.where(t > 0, t, 0.5 * t)
    print(sc, round(m.mean()), "W mean,", round(t.max()), "W peak,", round(max(-t.min(), 0)), "W regen peak")
```
