Before you start

about 2 hours: set up the gantry and read this page

You need
  • drill press + 22 mm Forstner bit
  • hacksaw
  • jigsaw
  • files
  • tap set M3–M8
  • hex keys
  • bike chain tool
  • luggage scale
  • multimeter
  • soldering iron
  • bench supply with a current limit
  • a laptop
Two people

One holds the e-stop and does nothing else, from the first powered motion.

E-stop first

The e-stop works before any motor is connected. It cuts every motor at once.

Always in the gantry

Chest and pelvis straps, rope just slack, for every powered test.

Batteries are the main hazard

Test every salvaged cell. Charge supervised, on concrete, never overnight.

The joints are strong

The knee pushes 335 N·m. Fingers out of chains and joints whenever power is on.

S1-Fast is a 1.75 m humanoid with legs driven by 12 hoverboard hub motors on bike chains, and hips and arms driven by 12 cordless-drill gearmotors. Everything is cut with hand tools and a drill press: no CNC, no 3D printer. Plan on about 220 hours of building, plus 2–4 weeks for parts to arrive.

Hoverboards

Six used ones give the 12 hub motors and most of the 52 battery cells. Broken boards are fine: you only need the motors and the packs.

Drills

Twelve 18 V drills with dead batteries: kerbside collections, tip shops, builders. All the same model if you can.

Bike parts

Old cassettes and chainrings give the 11–18T and 26–42T sprockets. Ask a bike co-op for worn-out bikes.

Order online

12 ODESC V4.2 drives (the biggest cost), BTS7960 H-bridges, MT6701 sensors, ESP32-S3 boards, CAN transceivers, the 60 A BMS.

Before you move on

All 12 motors spin freely with no rubbing and read within 15 % of the table at 50 A.

4

Make the chain drives

about 30 hours

You need
  • bike cassettes and chainrings
  • bike chain (about 12 m)
  • chain tool
  • 8 mm steel rod or M8 bolts
  • 608 bearings
  • 12 mm plywood

Each hub-motor joint has two chain stages. The motor's small sprocket drives a big one on a jackshaft; a small one on the jackshaft drives the big sprocket bolted to the joint. Multiply the two size ratios to get the joint's gearing.

Tooth counts are what the simulation sized; nearby sizes work if you re-run the selection.

Before you move on

With the motors unpowered, turning the joint by hand turns both motors smoothly, with no tight spots and no chain jumping when you push hard on the link.

7

Wire it up

about 25 hours

You need
  • 12 ODESC drives
  • 12 BTS7960 H-bridges
  • 16+ MT6701 sensors + magnets
  • TCA9548A multiplexers
  • 2 ESP32-S3
  • CAN transceivers
  • 120 Ω resistors
Careful

Do not put all 12 drives on one CAN bus: 500 torque commands a second to each drive would overload it.

9

Set up the drives and the software

about 20 hours

You need
  • laptop with Python
  • odrivetool
  • USB cables
  • the repository

Each ODESC (once per drive)

# odrivetool, ODrive firmware 0.5.x on the ODESC V4.2. Names follow ODrive's 0.5.x
# hoverboard guide: check them against the firmware your drive shipped with.
odrv0.config.brake_resistance = 2.0
odrv0.config.gpio9_mode = GPIO_MODE_DIGITAL      # hall inputs
odrv0.config.gpio10_mode = GPIO_MODE_DIGITAL
odrv0.config.gpio11_mode = GPIO_MODE_DIGITAL
odrv0.axis0.motor.config.pole_pairs = 15
odrv0.axis0.motor.config.resistance_calib_max_voltage = 4
odrv0.axis0.motor.config.requested_current_range = 60
odrv0.axis0.motor.config.current_control_bandwidth = 100
odrv0.axis0.motor.config.calibration_current = 10
odrv0.axis0.motor.config.current_lim = 50
odrv0.axis0.motor.config.torque_constant = 0.48  # your lever-test figure
odrv0.axis0.encoder.config.mode = ENCODER_MODE_HALL
odrv0.axis0.encoder.config.cpr = 90
odrv0.axis0.encoder.config.calib_scan_distance = 150
odrv0.axis0.encoder.config.bandwidth = 100
odrv0.axis0.controller.config.control_mode = CONTROL_MODE_TORQUE_CONTROL
odrv0.axis0.config.can.node_id = 1               # 1 to 6 on each leg
odrv0.can.config.baud_rate = 1000000
odrv0.save_configuration()

Then calibrate with the motor free to turn: motor calibration, then encoder offset calibration, and set both pre_calibrated flags.

Stand, step, walk

about 30 hours of careful testing

You need
  • gantry
  • second person on the e-stop
  • gait_W0.csv
  • the walking policy
  1. HangFeet off the ground. Move each joint slowly to the first row of gait_W0.csv.Holds the pose, no shaking
  2. StandFeet down, rope taking most of the weight. Crouch to the standing pose.5 minutes; motors warm, not hot
  3. ShiftRope slack. Move the weight from foot to foot with hip roll and ankle roll.Stays balanced
  4. Step slowlyPlay gait_W0.csv at quarter, half, then full speed. It is balanced at any speed.Steps without loading the rope
  5. Policy, gentlySwitch to the walking policy at 30 % current limits, commanded 0.3 m/s.Rope stays slack
  6. FasterRaise the command 0.2 m/s at a time, and the current limits back to 100 %.Repeatable, no falls into the rope
  7. Design speed1.0 m/s, the speed the joints were sized for. Past it only with the e-stop in hand.You have a walking robot

The scripted motions live in scrap/s1fast/data/. Do not replay gait_W1.csv or gait_W2.csv slower than real time: they balance dynamically and tip over when slowed.

When something goes wrong

What you seeLikely causeFix
ODESC calibration fails or the motor stuttersHall wires in the wrong order, or the wrong pole-pair countSwap two hall signal wires; check pole pairs = 15
A chain jumps a tooth under loadToo much slack, or the sprockets are not in lineRe-tension to 5 mm slack; line up the sprockets with a straight edge
A joint shakes when holding a posekp too high, or slack in the chainHalve kp, then raise it slowly; take out the slack
Everything cuts out when the robot stepsBMS overcurrent tripUse a 60 A BMS; check the main fuse is 60 A
A drill joint slips in normal walkingClutch set too lowRe-set it on the lever rig to the table value
A motor gets hotHolding a crouch too long, or a joint fighting its stopStand straighter between tests; check the soft limits
The two motors of a joint fight each otherOne is reversed, or different sprocketsFlip one motor's direction in the config; match the first-stage sprockets
