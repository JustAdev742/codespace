## How to read it

Every line records:
- manufacturer and part number, quantity, unit price in the seller's currency, AUD conversion;
- **make/buy class**: buy, machine (CNC job shop), print (MJF), custom (outsourced to a drawing), make (you fabricate);
- **price status**:
  - **V**: price seen on a manufacturer/retailer page during research;
  - **Q**: quote-only part, so the price is an estimate;
  - **E**: engineering estimate;
- the Australian supplier or source, an alternative part, and whether it is **baseline** (needed) or an **option**.

FX assumptions (AUD per unit): USD 1.52, EUR 1.65, GBP 1.95, CHF 1.75, CNY 0.21. **Update before ordering.**

## Alternatives built into the BOM

* Drives: custom F28P65x/GaN drive (TIDA-010979 pattern) instead of purchased modules, saving ≈ A$20k at the cost of months of firmware and safety work (R2 recommendation: buy first).
* Encoders: MT6835 magnetic (≈ A$18) instead of AksIM-2 on the motor side, already used on 19 joints.
* Strain-wave: Harmonic Drive CSG (premium, ≈ 2–3×) or Leaderdrive (baseline).
* Hands: Inspire RH56DFTP commercial hand (option line) instead of the custom hand.
* F/T sensors: Bota SensONE T5 / PixONE T60 (option lines) instead of estimating wrench from joint torques.

## Small parts are included

Fasteners (12.9, A4, Ti), dowels, thread-locker, thermal epoxy, grease, connectors, wire,
crimps, labels, filters, bumpers, soles, fingertip caps and a 10 % spares allowance all have
BOM lines. If you find a part on a drawing that has no BOM line, it falls under B081
(fasteners & consumables). Add it to `calc/kestrel/bom.py` so the next roll-up is complete.
